## 01. The script-reading trick

Vision models post impressive scores on chart-reading benchmarks, often answering questions about bar graphs and line charts with near-perfect accuracy.

The catch is in the test data: most benchmarks test models on clean plots generated by simple Python scripts using Matplotlib or Seaborn defaults.

Because frontier models are trained on huge amounts of public code, these charts are familiar territory. The models recognize the default fonts, tick spacing, and standard color palettes. High scores here mostly prove that an AI can recognize common code libraries, not that it can reason about scientific data. Test the same model on actual published papers from _Nature_ or a clinical oncology trial, and its performance drops.

> _"Scientific figures are visual arguments that rely on specialized shorthand, not simple spreadsheets turned into graphics."_

---

## 02. What clean plots leave out

Programmatic chart generators focus on neat presentation: labels never collide, ticks are predictable, and colors follow high-contrast presets.

Real research operates under practical publication constraints:

- Authors cram data points into limited page space and break axes to display extreme outliers.
- Critical details hide in margin notes and inset panels.
- Visual marks carry field-specific meanings. In oncology, small vertical ticks on a survival curve indicate censored patient data. In materials science, dashed lines often represent theoretical boundaries rather than missing numbers. In microbiology, an abrupt plateau can indicate instrument saturation rather than biological limits.

Synthetic datasets treat these marks as plain lines and dots. But to a researcher, they change the entire conclusion. If a benchmark strips out those conventions, it tests whether an AI can measure pixels, not whether it understands research.

---

## 03. Testing real research documents

On synthetic plots, models can usually answer questions just by running text extraction on tidy axis labels. They never have to decipher what an irregular visual layout actually means.

| Dimension          | Synthetic Benchmark Plots        | Published Scientific Figures                |
| :----------------- | :------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------ |
| **Data**           | Random clean distributions       | Experimental measurements with noise        |
| **Layout**         | Automated margins, no collisions | Crammed notes, split axes, insets           |
| **Symbols**        | Generic geometric coordinates    | Field-specific clinical and lab conventions |
| **Model Shortcut** | Reading clear labels with OCR    | Requires understanding the experiment       |

If multimodal models are going to help scientists review trial data or audit literature, we need benchmarks built from actual papers. Until evaluations use the messy, compact figures researchers actually read, high benchmark scores remain an illusion of competence.
