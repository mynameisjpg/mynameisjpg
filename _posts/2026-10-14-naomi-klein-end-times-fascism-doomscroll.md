## 01. Archival Summary

In her book _End Times Fascism_, co-authored with Astra Taylor, Naomi Klein defines the term as **a coalition of three main factions (religious, tech, and ethno-nationalist)** that are united by apocalyptic narratives (0:40–6:00). She argues that these groups, while distinct, are all mobilized by themes of terminal crisis and are essentially "prepping" the nation-state for the end of the world (0:40–7:05).

### Key components of this alliance include:

• `Theocratic Fundamentalists`: Religious groups who interpret current global crises and shocks as foretold signs of the end days, often adopting an accelerationist view where such events are welcomed (0:40–1:40).

• `Techno-Capitalists`: Figures in the tech industry whose rhetoric surrounding AI—such as summoning a "digital god" or anticipating super-intelligence—mirrors messianic eschatology (2:49–5:10).

• `Ethno-Nationalists`: A mass movement focused on "fortressing" borders, expelling perceived outsiders, and securing resources for the in-group within a national "bunker" (5:50–7:05).

Klein frames this phenomenon as "capitalism with its teeth bared," suggesting that neoliberalism's failure to address systemic crises has created the conditions for this authoritarian response to gain traction (7:45–8:45). Ultimately, she describes their policy goals and foreign policy as a form of "supersized prepping" at the level of the nation-state (6:57–7:05).
