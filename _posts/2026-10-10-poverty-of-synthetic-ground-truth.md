## 01. The synthetic benchmark illusion

On popular vision-language benchmarks like FigureQA and DVQA, modern multimodal models post scores above 80% or 90%. They seem remarkably competent at reading bar graphs, parsing trends, and extracting numbers.

The catch is in how those benchmarks are built. Most rely on synthetic plots generated by simple Python scripts using Matplotlib or Seaborn defaults, paired with repetitive question templates.

Because large models are pre-trained on vast amounts of public code, these figures are familiar territory. They recognize standard fonts, default tick intervals, and preset color palettes. Minor changes expose how fragile that recognition is: in stress tests conducted by the CharXiv research team, slightly altering the wording of a question caused models like SPHINX V2 to plunge by 34.5% on the exact same charts. High scores mostly reflected memorized plotting templates rather than actual visual reasoning.

> _"Scientific figures are visual arguments that rely on specialized shorthand, not simple spreadsheets turned into graphics."_

---

## 02. What happens on real research: The CharXiv test

To test how models handle genuine scientific documents, researchers created **CharXiv**, collecting 2,323 real figures across 8 disciplines directly from arXiv preprints.

The results showed a stark drop in performance compared to synthetic leaderboards:

- On visual reasoning questions, human participants scored **80.5%**, GPT-4o managed **47.1%**, and the best open-source model (InternVL Chat V1.5) achieved only **29.2%**. Most other open-source models scored below 25%.
- Even on basic perceptual tasks, models failed in surprising ways. When asked to simply count the labeled tick marks along an axis, a trivial task for humans (92.9% accuracy), 20 out of 24 models scored under 10%, performing barely better than random guessing.
- When questions asked about features that did not exist in a subplot, models routinely hallucinated answers instead of recognizing that the element was absent.

## 03. What code generators strip away

Synthetic chart generators prioritize visual legibility: labels never collide, ticks are evenly spaced, and curves follow clean mathematical distributions.

Published research operates under practical printing constraints:

- Authors cram dense data points into multi-panel layouts, breaking axes to display extreme outliers alongside baselines.
- Critical details sit in margin annotations, insets, and shared legends.
- Visual marks carry field-specific conventions. In oncology, small vertical ticks on a survival curve indicate censored patients. In materials science, dashed lines often represent theoretical boundaries rather than missing data. In microbiology, an abrupt plateau can indicate instrument saturation rather than biological carrying capacity.

Synthetic datasets treat these marks as abstract geometry. But to a researcher, they alter the entire conclusion.

| Dimension              | Synthetic Benchmarks (FigureQA, DVQA) | Authentic Research Figures (CharXiv)                        |
| :--------------------- | :------------------------------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Data source**        | Random mathematical distributions     | Real experimental measurements with noise                   |
| **Layout**             | Uniform margins, automated spacing    | Multi-panel subplots, split axes, crowded insets            |
| **Visual symbols**     | Generic coordinate geometry           | Discipline-specific shorthand (censoring ticks, boundaries) |
| **Model shortcut**     | Reading clear labels with OCR         | Requires understanding the experimental setup               |
| **Reasoning accuracy** | Often 80% to 90%+                     | Drops to 47.1% (GPT-4o) and <30% (open-source)              |

If multimodal models are going to help scientists review trial data or audit literature, evaluation suites must reflect the complex figures researchers actually publish. Until benchmarks move past tidy synthetic plots, high chart-reading scores will remain an illusion.
