## 01. The Goal Gradient Effect:

Humans are more motivated to complete tasks when they can visualize progress. Apps should use elements like progress bars or streaks to keep users engaged. Examples include progress bars during onboarding, streaks in apps like Duolingo, or displaying specific numbers like "5 of 7 tasks completed" rather than just a generic "complete this task".

## 02. The Peak-End Rule:

Users tend to judge an experience primarily by its most intense moments (the "peak") and how it ends, rather than the average of the whole experience. Positive reinforcement on success screens is crucial. Apps leverage this by focusing on success screens or positive reinforcement upon completing a difficult task, ensuring the final memory of the interaction is positive.
<img src="assets/images/5psych-2.png" width="100%"/>

---

## 03. The Labor Illusion:

People place more value on things that appear to take effort. Adding a slight, artificial wait time for complex tasks (like background checks or data loading) can make an app feel more trustworthy and premium. Apps use this by adding a deliberate delay to tasks like validating a driver’s license or running a background check—even if the results are ready instantly—to show that the system is "working" on the user's behalf, which increases trust.
<img src="assets/images/5psych-3.png" width="100%"/>

---

## 04. The Von Restorff Effect:

The brain better remembers items that stand out from their surroundings. Designers should use unique icons or visual highlights to draw attention to important calls-to-action. In UI design, this means using unique icons, bold colors, or specific illustrations to make the most important buttons or actions stand out from the rest of the interface.
<img src="assets/images/5psych-4.png" width="100%"/>

---

## 05. Choice Overload:

Too many options can cause user anxiety and decision fatigue. Simplifying navigation through categorization, filtering, and removing unnecessary elements helps users feel more in control. Successful apps combat this by offering filtering and search tools, clear categorization, or by removing unnecessary navigation elements altogether to simplify the user's decision-making process.
